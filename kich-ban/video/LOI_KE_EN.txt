0.1|One evening, in a small village in northern Vietnam,
0.2|two men died within minutes of each other. One was the richest, most powerful man in the village. The other was the man the whole village was most afraid of.
0.3|But the whole thing started because of... / a pot of rice porridge.
0.4|Yeah, seriously. One pot of porridge. With scallions.
0.5|A baby gets found inside an abandoned brick kiln.
0.6|A grown man slashes his own face open, just to get paid one silver coin.
0.7|The ugliest woman in the village does something for him that nobody has done in forty years.
0.8|And every single day, he screams insults at the entire village. And nobody ever screams back.
0.9|So, how did one pot of porridge end with two bodies? / Let's start with the screaming.
1.1|Now, it's the nineteen thirties, in a village called Vũ Đại. Right here.
1.2|A man is walking down the road, cursing. He curses the sky. He curses his life.
1.3|He curses the whole village of Vũ Đại. Then he curses whoever gave birth to him.
1.4|Nobody answers. Not because they can't hear him. Because to them, he's not worth answering.
1.5|So, who is this guy?
1.6|His name is Chí Phèo, and basically, he belongs to nobody. A man out setting eel traps found him as a newborn, naked and grey with cold, inside an old abandoned brick kiln.
1.7|Right here, at the edge of the village. Far from the houses. Nobody around. / Remember that kiln. It matters later.
1.8|The baby got handed to a blind widow. She sold him to a childless man who carved rice mortars. Then the mortar maker died. And the boy got passed from house to house, working for food.
1.9|At twenty, he becomes a farmhand for a man named Kiến. Later, everyone calls him Bá Kiến. Bá basically means big-shot landlord. That's his house, right there. Biggest one in the village.
1.10|And back then, Chí is gentle. Really gentle. Harmless as dirt, the story says. / Then the landlord's third wife calls him in to rub her legs.
1.11|Bà Ba, the third wife, is lounging on a daybed, fanning herself. Chí, get in here and rub my legs. / He mumbles that he hasn't finished plowing. / I said, get in here.
1.12|Bá Kiến notices. He pours his tea and goes: so, our farmhand's been spending a lot of time in the back room lately. / His son asks: want me to fire him? / Firing him's too nice. The district's looking for people to send to jail.
1.13|Nobody asks what Chí actually did. He didn't do anything. The story just says people figured Bá Kiến was jealous. Either way, a twenty-year-old farmhand gets marched off to the district, and into prison. / The charge? A rich man was in a bad mood.
2.1|Seven or eight years later, Chí comes back.
2.2|Nobody recognizes him.
2.3|Head shaved bald. Teeth scraped white. Face dark and hard. Eyes that glare at everything. A shirt hanging open over a chest covered in dragon and phoenix tattoos, plus a general swinging a war club.
2.4|Seven or eight years. That's a kid getting through all of elementary school. And it's enough to turn the gentlest farmhand in the village into something else entirely.
2.5|So, what did prison do to him? You find out on day one. / He drinks.
2.6|Then he walks straight to Bá Kiến's front gate. Right here. And stands there, yelling.
2.7|Bá Kiến! Get out here! You put me in prison, and guess what? I'm back!
2.8|The son, Lý Cường, rolls up his sleeves and tells the servants to go give the drunk a beating.
2.9|So Chí smashes his liquor bottle. / And with the broken glass, he cuts his own face.
2.10|Then he drops into the dirt and starts howling. Help! Somebody help me! The landlord and his son are murdering me!
2.11|The neighbors show up, but they keep their distance. Their take: not our house, not our problem.
2.12|And then Bá Kiến strolls out, sweet as honey. Chí! Is that you? Why didn't you come inside? Who did this to you? / Chí just sits there, breathing hard. He's got nothing.
2.13|We're all family here, Bá Kiến says. Go on, take this silver coin, and get yourself a drink.
2.14|They were not family. / One silver coin. A self-slashed face, traded for one silver coin. And Chí takes it. / That's all Bá Kiến needed to know. A guy who curses you is dangerous. A guy who curses you and then takes your money? That's a knife. You just have to hold it by the handle.
2.15|And remember those glass cuts on his face. They're going to follow him for the rest of his life. Literally.
3.1|Now, it's the years after that. So, why is the whole village terrified of him? Because Bá Kiến gave him a job.
3.2|Bá Kiến isn't a good man. But he's a smart one. He knows people here fear exactly one thing: a man with nothing left to lose. And now, he owns one.
3.3|Chí, the soldier Tảo still owes me money. Go collect. / And if he doesn't pay? / Then do it your way. / You're not worried I'll stab him? / Bá Kiến looks up and smiles. I'm worried you won't.
3.4|His son is nervous. You raise a dog like that, someday it bites back. / Bá Kiến flips through his debt book. A dog needs a chain. Give it a little land. Problem solved.
3.5|So Bá Kiến gives him five sào of riverside garden. A sào is an old unit of land. Five of them is about the size of a small soccer field. It's the only thing Chí will ever own.
3.6|What he doesn't realize is that garden is the chain. And one day, he's going to see it for exactly what it is.
3.7|From then on, Chí is the landlord's muscle. He stabs for hire. He smashes up families, wrecks livelihoods, and makes a lot of honest people bleed and cry.
3.8|He's drunk all the time. He eats drunk, sleeps drunk, hurts people drunk. He can't even remember how old he is.
3.9|The village gives him a nickname. The demon of Vũ Đại.
3.10|And honestly? The name fits. People just forgot who raised the demon.
4.1|Now, it's a night with a full moon.
4.2|So, what kind of woman would get anywhere near this guy? / Thị Nở.
4.3|The author, Nam Cao, doesn't go easy on her. Uglier than a ghost, dirt poor, a little simple, and from a family the village avoids like a curse. Nobody wants to be near her. Same as nobody wants to be near Chí.
4.4|Two people the whole village has pushed out. And tonight, they're separated by one row of banana trees.
4.5|Thị Nở goes down to the river for water, right by Chí's banana garden. It's hot. The river breeze is cool. She sits down against a banana tree to rest for a minute.
4.6|And falls asleep.
4.7|Chí stumbles out into the garden, drunk. And he sees her.
4.8|The story covers this part fast. By morning, everything is different. / Just not the way you'd think.
5.1|Now, it's the next morning. So, what changed?
5.2|Last night, Chí caught a chill. He threw up. And this morning, for the first time in years, he's sober.
5.3|He hears birds outside.
5.4|People laughing on their way to market.
5.5|A fisherman knocking his oar against his boat to scare fish into the net.
5.6|Those sounds were there every single day. Today is just the first time he hears them.
5.7|And he remembers something he'd forgotten. When he was young, he had one tiny dream. A little family. He'd hire out to plow fields. His wife would weave cloth. They'd raise one pig to save up. And if things went well, they'd buy a few patches of rice field.
5.8|That's it. That was the whole dream. Now he's past forty. Over the hill. And still completely alone.
5.9|Then Thị Nở walks in. She's carrying a pot of hot scallion porridge.
5.10|She says, you were sick last night, so I made you some porridge. / For me? he asks. / Eat it while it's hot. Scallion porridge sweats the fever out. / Chí doesn't say anything for a long time. Then, quietly: nobody's ever cooked for me before.
5.11|And that's the truth. Forty years. No woman has ever handed him anything. Anything he wanted, he had to threaten for, steal, or slash his own face for. This time, he didn't have to do a thing. / His eyes get a little wet. And the porridge tastes amazing.
5.12|Nam Cao sums him up in one short line. He craves being a decent man. He wants to make peace with everyone. And he thinks Thị Nở is his way back in.
5.13|For five days, they live together like husband and wife. / For the first time in his life, the demon of Vũ Đại doesn't want a drink.
6.1|Five days later. Thị Nở suddenly remembers she has an aunt. She figures she'd better ask her first.
6.2|So, who slams that door shut? Not Bá Kiến. Not the district officials. An old woman, a few lanes over. Right here.
6.3|Auntie, I want to marry Chí. / The aunt's eyes go wide. That guy? The one whose only job is slashing his face to shake people down? / He's gentle now, Auntie. / Gentle? Has anyone in this village ever seen him gentle? You marry him, and the whole village will laugh you into the ground.
6.4|He was gentle now. Nobody got the chance to see it. And nobody planned to stick around and find out. The village would laugh. That was the aunt's entire argument. And it was enough.
6.5|Thị Nở storms back and dumps the aunt's exact words on Chí. Then she walks out.
6.6|He runs after her and grabs her hand. She shoves him off. He falls flat in the dirt.
6.7|Five days. That's Chí Phèo's entire life as a decent man. / One work week. Not counting the weekend.
7.1|Now, it's that same afternoon. Chí is drinking again. But here's the strange part. The more he drinks, the more sober he gets.
7.2|He catches the smell of scallion porridge. And he breaks down sobbing.
7.3|Then he tucks a knife into his belt, and heads out.
7.4|So, who's he going to stab? He mutters it the whole way. I'll kill that old hag. Meaning Thị Nở's aunt.
7.5|But his feet don't take him to the aunt's house. They take him straight to Bá Kiến's gate. The exact same road he walked on his first day out of prison.
7.6|Bá Kiến doesn't even look up. Drunk again, Chí? Out of money? / I didn't come for money. I want to be a decent man. / Bá Kiến smirks. Sure. I'll give you...
7.7|No! Chí screams. Who's going to let me be decent? How do I get rid of these scars on my face? I can't be decent anymore. / There's only one way out.
7.8|He swings the knife.
7.9|Bá Kiến dies right there. / Then Chí turns the knife on himself.
7.10|Remember when I said those glass cuts would follow him? Bá Kiến bought them for one silver coin. Then traded them for a garden. But nothing in that house could buy them back. The one person who offered Chí a way home closed the door after five days. And the man who pushed him off that road in the first place was sitting right in front of him. / He didn't take the wrong road.
8.1|One evening. Two bodies. Bá Kiến was killed by the knife he'd spent years sharpening. Chí Phèo died trying to become the man he used to be.
8.2|Thị Nở kept her reputation with her aunt. And lost the only man who ever cried over her porridge.
8.3|And the village of Vũ Đại? Finally, everybody had something to say. Serves them right.
8.4|This story isn't saying bad people are born bad. It's showing how a gentle man gets turned into a demon. And how, when the demon tries to become human again, a whole village slams the door.
8.5|Nam Cao wrote this in nineteen forty-one. His original title was The Old Brick Kiln. The publisher renamed it A Well-Matched Couple. It didn't get the name we know, Chí Phèo, until nineteen forty-six.
8.6|And that first title wasn't random. Because when Thị Nở hears that Chí is dead, she glances down at her belly.
8.7|And she thinks of an old abandoned brick kiln. Far from the houses. Nobody around. / Remember when I said that kiln would matter?
8.8|And the whole thing started because of one pot of porridge. And maybe, it's about to start again. / Same kiln.
