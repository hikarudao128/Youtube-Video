### 0. COLD OPEN

**[0:00]**
🎙 NARRATION:
One afternoon, in a village in northern Vietnam, a family of six lost half a month of food. In one lunch.
But the whole thing started because a man wanted a drink.
Yeah, seriously. One drink. Okay, a lot of drinks.
A man sells the same banana trees twice. Four kids help kill their own dog, and they're excited about it. A mother sits in the kitchen and tells her children to wait for the leftovers. And then the tray comes down from the table.
So, how did one drink end with five people crying over an empty tray? Let's start with the bananas.

### 1. THE BANANA TREES

**[0:45]**
🎙 NARRATION:
Now, it's the early 1940s. Vietnam is a French colony, and in the countryside, a lot of people are hungry. Not "skipped lunch" hungry. Hungry hungry.
This is the house. Right here. Mud walls, a straw roof, a little yard, a bamboo fence around it.
So, who is this guy?
That's the father. Right here, on the bed, at noon. Nam Cao never gives him a name. The story just calls him "hắn". Basically, "him". "That guy".
His wife? Same deal. The story calls her "thị". Basically, "her".
And the kids, over here. Four of them. Cái Gái, which just means "the girl". She's the oldest. Then cu Nhớn, "the big boy". Cu Nhỡ, "the middle boy". And cu Con, "the little one".
Nobody in this family gets a real name. The kids are basically numbered.
Here's the problem. The father loves two things: rượu, which is cheap rice liquor, and gambling. He does not love working.
So when he needs money, he sells stuff. And when he runs out of stuff, he gets creative.

🗣 SKIT #1:
[FATHER]: Mụ Vụ! Ten young banana trees. Two đồng. Today only.
[MỤ VỤ]: Ten? They're healthy?
[FATHER]: Best in the village.
[MỤ VỤ]: Fine. Two đồng.
[FATHER]: Pleasure doing business.
(beat)
[FATHER]: …Hey, neighbor! Ten young banana trees. Interested?
[NEIGHBOR]: Aren't those the ones you just sold?
[FATHER]: Nobody's going to check.

🎙 NARRATION:
Somebody checked.
He sold the same ten banana trees to two different people. The two đồng, the old Vietnamese money, went straight into xóc đĩa. That's a gambling game where you shake coins under a bowl and bet on how they land. The two đồng didn't come back.
Not a fortune. Just enough for one bad afternoon.
So now mụ Vụ will never buy anything from him again. The shop won't give him credit. Nobody in the village will lend him a cent.
He's out of bananas. He's out of friends with money. And he's very, very thirsty.

🎬 VISUALS: Hand-drawn map of the hamlet: the house "right here", mụ Vụ's house "over here", the market "down here". The banana grove, ten saplings, a big "10" on screen. Two coins flying from his hand onto a xóc đĩa bowl. Stamp over the shop door: "NO CREDIT".
🎵 AUDIO: Light, plucky music for the skit; a record-scratch on "Somebody checked."

### 2. THE DOG BY THE FENCE

**[2:10]**
🎙 NARRATION:
Now, it's one morning. His wife has gone to the market. The kids are in the yard.
So, what do you do when nobody will lend you a cent?
You look around the house for something that's still worth money.
And there it is. Right here, by the fence. The family dog. Asleep in the sun.
The story doesn't give the dog a name either. It doesn't need one. Everybody in the house knows exactly which dog it is.
And the father starts doing what he does best. Not working. Explaining.

🗣 SKIT #2:
[FATHER]: Okay. Think about it logically.
[FATHER]: The dog eats rice. We don't have rice.
[FATHER]: So really, the dog is costing us money.
[FATHER]: And a dog that costs money is basically a debt.
[FATHER]: And you pay off debts.
[FATHER]: With rượu. And galangal. And shrimp paste.
[FATHER]: It's basically an investment.

🎙 NARRATION:
It was not an investment.
But he's already decided. He just needed the speech so he could feel like a guy who thinks things through.
And he doesn't do it alone. He calls the kids over to help.
Here's the part that hurts. The kids are thrilled. They hold on. They run back and forth. Because in their heads, this is simple math: a dog in the pot means meat. Meat means a real meal. For once.
Nam Cao doesn't make them monsters. They're hungry kids who've been told there's going to be a feast.
And the kids were cheering. That was the last time anyone in this family cheered.

### 3. THE MARKET

**[3:50]**
🎙 NARRATION:
A few hours later, the mother comes home from the market.
So, what does the dog's owner think?
Because make no mistake. In this house, she's the one who keeps everybody alive. She's the one who scrapes together every meal.
She walks through the gate. She looks at the fence. And it's empty.
In her head, that dog was worth half a month of rice. Rice for the whole family. Fifteen days of six people not going to bed hungry.
Half a month of rice. Remember that number. It matters later.

🗣 SKIT #3:
[MOTHER]: Where's the dog?
[FATHER]: Relax.
[MOTHER]: That dog was half a month of rice.
[FATHER]: And now it's lunch. Same thing.
[MOTHER]: It's not the same thing.
[FATHER]: I invited a few guys over. Respect, you know? We owe people.
[MOTHER]: We owe everybody.
[FATHER]: Exactly. So this fixes it. And there'll be plenty for everyone.

🎙 NARRATION:
There would not be plenty for everyone.
And you can see her do the math, right here, standing in the yard. She can't bring the dog back. She can't sell it now. Arguing won't feed anybody.
So she does what she always does. She swallows it. And she goes to the kitchen.
Nobody said it was fair.

🎬 VISUALS: The mother at the gate, a basket on her arm. Slow push-in on the empty fence. On-screen: "1 DOG = 15 DAYS OF RICE", fifteen small bowls lining up, then all of them disappearing at once. Split screen for the skit: him relaxed on the bed, her standing.

### 4. THE FEAST

**[5:00]**
🎙 NARRATION:
Now, it's lunchtime.
Three guests show up. Right here, around the bamboo table. Three men from the village, plus the father. Four grown men. One dog.
And the food comes out.
Nồi xáo. That's dog meat stewed in a pot with spices. It's steaming. The whole house smells like it.
Two bowls of tiết canh. That's raw blood pudding, set like jelly, with herbs and peanuts on top. It's a party dish. A rich man's dish.
And rượu. A lot of rượu.
Nam Cao writes this part almost like a love letter to the food. The smell goes everywhere. And that's the point. Because it goes into the kitchen, too.
So, where's everybody else?
Right here. The kitchen corner. Behind the wall. The mother, thin and worn out, and four skinny kids, sitting on the floor in the smoke.
They can hear the cups. They can smell the meat. They can't see any of it.

🗣 SKIT #4 (serious):
[CU CON]: Mom. It smells good.
[MOTHER]: I know.
[CU NHỠ]: When do we eat?
[MOTHER]: Soon.
[CU NHỚN]: How soon?
[MOTHER]: Let them finish first.
[MOTHER]: Whatever's left, that's ours.
[CU CON]: Will there be a lot left?
[MOTHER]: …Just wait.

🎙 NARRATION:
"Whatever's left, that's ours."
And that sentence was going to sit in that kitchen all afternoon.
Out front, the lunch keeps going. Another bowl. Another cup. Another bowl. Another cup.
One o'clock. Two o'clock. The men are red in the face and loud. Somebody's telling a story. Somebody's laughing too hard at it.
In the kitchen, nobody's telling stories.
The story doesn't say whether the father thinks about them even once. Either way, he doesn't get up.

### 5. THE TRAY

**[7:00]**
🎙 NARRATION:
Now, it's late afternoon.
So, what's left?
The father finally calls out. He calls cái Gái, the oldest, to come and clear the tray.
And for a second, it's fun again. She carries the tray toward the kitchen, and she teases her little brothers with it. Holding it up. Making them wait. Like a big sister does.
The boys crowd around her.
She puts the tray down.
Empty bowls.
That's it. Empty bowls.
Remember half a month of rice? Four men ate it in one afternoon. And left the bowls.
Cu Con, the little one, throws himself on the floor. Rolling, screaming, crying. The way little kids cry when it's not about the food anymore. It's about being promised.
And then the mother cries.
She doesn't yell. She doesn't go out there. She just cries.
And one by one, the other kids cry with her.
Five people. One kitchen. And out front, four men who aren't hungry anymore.

### 6. ENDING

**[8:20]**
🎙 NARRATION:
So, let's add it up.
The dog is gone. The banana trees are gone, twice. Half a month of rice is gone in one lunch.
The father got his drink. He got his respect from three guys who went home full.
The kids helped kill their own dog, and didn't get a single bite of it.
The mother kept the family alive for years, and couldn't keep one dog alive for one morning.
Nam Cao wrote a lot of stories about hunger. But this one isn't really about not having food. There was food. Right there, on the table. It's about who gets to eat it. And what hunger does to a man until he stops seeing his own kids.
And the whole thing started because a man wanted a drink.
Remember the fence? The spot where the dog used to sleep in the sun? It's empty now. Nobody in the story says a word about it.
The title says children don't eat dog meat.
Neither, it turns out, do mothers.
