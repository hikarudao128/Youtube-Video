One evening in a small Vietnamese village, two men died within minutes of each other: the richest man in the village, and the man everyone feared most. And the whole thing started because of a pot of scallion porridge.

This is the entire story of Chí Phèo, Nam Cao's 1941 classic and one of the most famous works of Vietnamese literature. A baby found in an abandoned brick kiln grows up to be a gentle farmhand, gets sent to prison on a rich man's whim, and comes back as "the demon of Vũ Đại." Then, for five days, someone shows him kindness, and he tries to become a decent man again.

Why did he kill the landlord who made him, on the very day he wanted to change?

ABOUT THE STORY
"Chí Phèo" was written by Nam Cao (1915–1951) and first published in 1941 under the title "Đôi lứa xứng đôi" (A Well-Matched Couple). Nam Cao's own title was "Cái lò gạch cũ" (The Old Brick Kiln), and the story took the name Chí Phèo in 1946. It is still taught in Vietnamese schools today.
